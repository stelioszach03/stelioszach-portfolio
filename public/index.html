01

BSc thesis
September 2026

Inverse problems · PyTorch · Medical imaging

When a generative prior
helps—and when it fails.

Score-based reconstruction of undersampled MRI, from faithful reproduction to uncertainty and domain shift.

I reimplemented a published MRI reconstruction solver in PyTorch, converted its pretrained JAX/Flax checkpoint, and studied reconstruction quality, sampling cost and the reliability of uncertainty estimates.

Within 0.15 dB

Thesis reports PSNR agreement with the reference on 256 BraTS test slices, across 4×, 8× and 24× undersampling.
Thesis, Table 5.1 ↗

Methods, findings & limitations

What I implemented

The inverse-problem pipeline, measurement operators, conditioning and data consistency, four solver modes, checkpoint conversion and experiment analysis. The original model architecture and pretrained prior are credited to the reference work.

What the experiments showed

Sample averaging improved reconstruction at additional sampling cost. Short adaptation was evaluated under domain shift, including a subject-disjoint OASIS-1 study. Uncertainty-guided acquisition did not outperform random selection under an equal extra-column budget.

What this does not establish

These are retrospective, single-coil reconstruction experiments on public imaging datasets. Uncertainty ranking is not a calibrated probability of failure. There was no prospective scanner study or clinical validation.

03

Current research
Ongoing · Unpublished

Model-based reinforcement learning

Can a world model tell us
which policy to trust?

Investigating the gap between imagined performance and what a policy actually does in an environment.

My current research studies imagination horizons, critic stability and policy selection in latent world models. I develop PyTorch experiments with controlled comparisons, paired evaluation contexts and explicit reporting of failed hypotheses.

  1. 01Learn latent dynamics
  2. 02Train through imagination
  3. 03Evaluate in a simulator
Current questions & evidence limits

Questions

When do uncertainty signals help assign an imagination horizon? How do critic targets affect learning? Do internal model scores reliably rank policies by environment performance?

What I have learned so far

The outcomes are task-dependent. Recent controlled studies include failed controller and selection hypotheses, rather than evidence of a universal improvement. Source-level diagnostics help distinguish learning-objective problems from prediction error.

Status

This is ongoing, unpublished research. Experiments use simulated control environments, not physical robots. Limited task, world-model and training-seed coverage constrains generalization.
