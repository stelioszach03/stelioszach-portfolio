BSc thesis
September 2026

Inverse problems · PyTorch · Medical imaging

When a generative prior
helps—and when it fails.

Score-based reconstruction of undersampled MRI, from faithful reproduction to uncertainty and domain shift.

I reimplemented a published MRI reconstruction solver in PyTorch, converted its pretrained JAX/Flax checkpoint, and studied reconstruction quality, sampling cost and the reliability of uncertainty estimates.

Within 0.15 dB

Thesis reports PSNR agreement with the reference on 256 BraTS test slices, across 4×, 8× and 24× undersampling.
Thesis, Table 5.1 ↗

Methods, findings & limitations

What I implemented

The inverse-problem pipeline, measurement operators, conditioning and data consistency, four solver modes, checkpoint conversion and experiment analysis. The original model architecture and pretrained prior are credited to the reference work.

What the experiments showed

Sample averaging improved reconstruction at additional sampling cost. Short adaptation was evaluated under domain shift, including a subject-disjoint OASIS-1 study. Uncertainty-guided acquisition did not outperform random selection under an equal extra-column budget.

What this does not establish

These are retrospective, single-coil reconstruction experiments on public imaging datasets. Uncertainty ranking is not a calibrated probability of failure. There was no prospective scanner study or clinical validation.

02

ForgeRL / ForgeBench
Agent routing · Evaluation

Reinforcement learning · Model routing · Sandboxed execution

Code repair with
an inspectable decision policy.

An authored benchmark and evidence dashboard for studying how a bounded coding agent allocates model calls after visible test feedback.

I built the multi-file repair harness, isolated executor, durable spending ledger and trajectory dashboard. Five policies compare fixed model use, escalation, hand-written rules and learned routing. The language models are not fine-tuned.

50 scenarios · 10 repository families

Miniature Python repositories with disjoint training, validation and test families. The v0.2 study completed 300 evaluation and 180 controller-training episodes. Inspect recorded prompts, supplied files, patches, test outcomes, retries, model switches, tokens and cost.

A separately frozen transfer pilot completed 162 episodes across 27 new miniature tasks and six policies. The simpler learned baseline solved 17/18 primary test tasks; fitted-Q, strong-only and escalation solved 16/18. One seed and six test families do not establish general superiority. All policies shared the same additional verification rule.

A later four-seed replication revisited the same, now-exposed catalog in 1,296 attempted episodes. On its original primary slice, strong-only solved 131/144 at lower accounted model cost than the fitted-Q router's 128/144. VERIFY-on and VERIFY-off each solved 372/432 attempts across policies. Four provider failures remain in the counts; this is not new-task generalization.

System, experiment & limitations

Bounded execution

Recorded replay is free to browse. An optional curated trial makes one GPT-OSS 20B request for a fixed task, with isolated verification and persistent spending limits. Live trials are separate from benchmark results; visitors cannot submit code or arbitrary prompts. Provider failures remain visible.

Policy comparison

Strong-only, cheap-only, escalation-on-failure, static and adaptive policies share a six-call, ten-decision cap. The controlled research profile routes GPT-OSS 20B and 120B through a fixed OpenRouter provider. Hidden checks grade the final candidate after all routing decisions.

Limits of the evidence

These are authored miniature repositories, not evidence of general repository repair or long-horizon autonomy. Sparse controller states use a declared fallback. The dashboard distinguishes actual coverage, provider charges and uncertain reservations; no general policy advantage is claimed.

04

MTA-Scan
Live data · Evaluation

Data engineering · FastAPI · Interactive maps

From subway observations
to an inspectable signal.

A working pipeline for exploring public NYC Subway observations, headway deviations and the limits of anomaly scores.

I implemented feed ingestion, the API and an interactive review workflow with freshness states, native Mapbox layers, filtering and explicit snapshot export. Autonomous history collection now supports chronological 15/30-minute evaluation against persistence and seasonal baselines, alongside the separate constructed replay.

Live observations
Inspect the network, filter a line or station and export the successful snapshot with its selected time window.
Visible failure states
Feed freshness and provider failures stay visible. A labelled map fallback preserves the data review workflow.
History & forecasting evidence
Inspect collection health, held-out errors and readiness. The target is future feed-predicted arrival spacing; early measurements are feasibility evidence.
Data & evaluation limits

What the live system establishes

Public-feed integration, bounded historical storage and automatic temporal evaluation. Forecasts require at least 14 days plus coverage and freshness checks. The target is a later arrival-estimate proxy, not actual train passages, passenger waiting time or validated incident prediction.

What the replay does not establish

The small replay contains three constructed scenarios, not official MTA incident ground truth or an independent benchmark. Higher recall in this fixture comes with more false alarms than the baselines. Neither the replay nor a working live map establishes validated incident prediction.

05

Inference Systems Lab
GPU serving · Performance

vLLM · RTX 4090 & A100 · Reproducible measurement

Measure the serving trade-offs.

Frozen GPU experiments connecting request traces to throughput, latency, device telemetry and output agreement.

I built a bounded streaming benchmark and fixed the model revision, container, workload and settings before execution. The first 1,536-request pilot on RTX 4090 was followed by a separate counterbalanced experiment across two model sizes, two GPU classes, prefix patterns, cache modes and four repeats.

16,384 measured requests · 2 GPUs

Qwen2.5 1.5B and 7B · vLLM 0.10.2 · RTX 4090 and A100 80GB. All requests completed at the HTTP level. Paired output hashes frequently differed, so throughput ratios do not establish lossless speedup or answer quality.

Measured results & limits

Observed throughput

At concurrency 32, the later study measured cached/uncached throughput ratios of 1.23–1.47 with shared prefixes across its GPU/model combinations; unique prefixes were near 1.00. Four-repeat means and ranges, latency and exact-output hash counts are in the report.

Scope of the comparison

Each hardware class used one allocated GPU and synthetic fixed-length prompts. Output text often differed. The $3.45 allocation cost is an estimate; exact provider billing is unavailable. Neither study measures answer quality, quantization or general production performance.
